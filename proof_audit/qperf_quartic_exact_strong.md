# A 50-bit quartic exact-strong candidate

**Status: proved.**  This file certifies a candidate replacement modulus and
challenge interface.  It is not the repository's current quadratic
`q_perf = 2^50-2687`, and it is not the manuscript's separate degree-eight
`q_exact = 2^50-351` theorem line.

The machine certificate is generated by
`verify_qperf_quartic_exact_strong.py` and stored at
`generated/qperf_quartic_exact_strong_certificate.json`.

## 1. Arithmetic certificate

Set

\[
 q_4=926510094425921=\frac{3^{32}+1}{2}.
\]

The complete-factorization Lucas certificate in the JSON proves that `q_4`
is prime.  In particular,

\[
 q_4-1=2^6\cdot5\cdot17\cdot41\cdot193\cdot21523361,
\]

and the certificate recursively proves the primality of every prime factor.
The verifier also checks

\[
 q_4\bmod256=65,\qquad \operatorname{ord}_{256}(q_4)=4,
 \qquad \operatorname{ord}_{q_4}(3)=64,
 \qquad 3^{32}=-1\pmod {q_4}.
\]

It then multiplies the claimed factors and checks the polynomial identity

\[
 X^{128}+1=\prod_{\substack{1\le u<64\\u\ {m odd}}}
              (X^4-3^u)\pmod {q_4}.
\]

Every root of one of the displayed quartics has order 256: if
`alpha^4=3^u` with odd `u`, then `alpha^128=-1`.  A primitive 256-th root has
degree `ord_256(q_4)=4` over `F_q4`.  Hence all 32 displayed quartics are
irreducible, distinct, and constitute the complete CRT factorization.

## 2. Signed radix-three form in every slot

Fix the slot `X^4-3^u`, where `u` is odd, and let `omega` be the residue of
`X`.  For

\[
 c(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{127}a_iX^i
\]

write

\[
 c(\omega)=\sum_{r=0}^3\omega^r U_r,
 \qquad
 U_r=\sum_{k=0}^{31}a_{4k+r}3^{uk}.
\]

Multiplication by odd `u` permutes the exponents modulo 32.  Whenever the
integer exponent crosses 32, `3^32=-1` supplies a sign.  Thus each `U_r` is a
signed permutation of a 32-digit radix-three sum

\[
 U_r=\sum_{e=0}^{31}b_e3^e.
\]

The four coordinates are separate because `1,omega,omega^2,omega^3` are an
`F_q4` basis of the quartic slot.

## 3. Exact-weight unit-difference theorem

**Theorem.**  Let `D_tau` be the uniform signed ternary vectors in degree 128
of exact Hamming weight `tau`.  For every `tau <= 32` and every two distinct
`c,c' in D_tau`, the difference `c-c'` is a unit of
`F_q4[X]/(X^128+1)`.  Negacyclic multiplication by a challenge has Euclidean
operator norm at most `tau`.

**Proof.**  Suppose the difference vanishes in one quartic slot.  After the
signed reindexing above, each of its four integer coordinate sums is

\[
 D_r=\sum_{e=0}^{31}d_{r,e}3^e,
 \qquad d_{r,e}\in\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}.
\]

Since `|D_r| <= 2 sum_e 3^e = 3^32-1 = 2q_4-2`, divisibility by `q_4`
forces `D_r` to be one of `-q_4,0,q_4`.

There are two elementary signed-digit facts:

1. the only 32-digit expansion of zero with digits in `[-2,2]` is the zero
   expansion;
2. every such expansion of `q_4` or `-q_4` has no zero digit and has an odd
   number of absolute-one digits.

For completeness, the second fact follows from the carry recurrence for
`Q_m=(3^m+1)/2` and `P_m=(3^m-1)/2`.  At a low digit, `Q_m` permits `-1` and
`2`, while `P_m` permits `1` and `-2`; choosing an absolute-two digit toggles
between the two states.  The terminal transition forces an odd number of
toggles.  Consequently all `m=32` digits are nonzero and both the
absolute-one and absolute-two counts are odd.  The verifier independently
enumerates this recurrence and asserts the complete profile set

\[
 \{(t_1,t_2):t_1+t_2=32,\ t_1\text{ odd}\}.
\]

Now interpret the digits as coefficient differences of two signed ternary
challenges.  An absolute-two digit forces both challenges to be nonzero at
that coefficient.  An absolute-one digit makes exactly one challenge
nonzero.  Therefore two equal-weight challenges require an even total number
of absolute-one digits and have forced per-challenge weight at least

\[
 t_2+\frac{t_1}{2}.
\]

If `k` of the four coordinate sums equal `+/-q_4`, each contributes an odd
`t_1`.  Odd `k` is incompatible with equal weights.  For `k=2`, the smallest
possible forced weight is 33; for `k=4`, it is 66.  Thus no nonzero vanishing
difference can arise when `tau <= 32`.  If all four sums are zero, every
difference digit is zero and `c=c'`, contrary to the hypothesis.  Hence the
difference is nonzero in every CRT slot and is a unit.

Finally, Young's convolution inequality gives

\[
 \|M_c\|_{2\to2}\le\|c\|_1=\tau.
\]

This is a uniform upper bound; equality is not asserted for every challenge.
`square`

### Tight fixed-weight boundary

The certificate contains explicit distinct weight-33 challenges whose
difference vanishes modulo `X^4-3`, and a common extra coefficient gives the
same failure at weight 34.  Thus this proof is intentionally restricted to
exact weights at most 32.  In particular, the repository's optional
`challenge-weight-34` line cannot be transferred to `q_4`.

The exact-weight-32 support is

\[
 |D_{32}|={128\choose32}2^{32},\qquad
 \log_2|D_{32}|=132.221300637359.
\]

For the repository's two-star final-query exposure `2LM`, the only unit-loss
term is challenge repetition:

\[
 \frac{2LM}{|D_{32}|}.
\]

It is at least 128 bits for `L<=8,M=1` and for `L<=4,M=2`.  At `L=8,M=2` it
is only 127.221300637359 bits, so exact weight 32 does not close that particular
ROM exposure.

## 4. Audit of the structured sampler proposal

The independently fixed residue-class weights `(10,10,10,9)` are also
rigorous.  For one residue class, balanced ternary maps signed weight-`w`
vectors injectively into the integer interval `[-(q_4-1),q_4-1]`; reduction
modulo `q_4` therefore has at most two preimages.  If

\[
 N_w={32\choose w}2^w,
 \]

then a fixed slot collision is at most `16/M_s`, where
`M_s=N_10^3 N_9`.  Exact challenge equality has probability `1/M_s` and is
contained in each of the 32 slot collision events.  Removing it in each slot
before the union bound gives the conditioned-distinct estimate

\[
 \kappa_{\rm nu}\le \frac{32(16-1)}{M_s-1}
                  =\frac{480}{M_s-1}.
\]

This slightly strengthens the supplied `511/(M_s-1)` bound.  The construction
has `gamma=39`, but under the current combined two-star exposure

\[
 2LM\left(\frac1{M_s}+\kappa_{\rm nu}\right)
\]

it gives only 127.660751356345 bits at `L=8,M=2`.  Therefore it does not close
that ledger.  The balanced weights `(10,10,10,10)` give `gamma=40` and
129.862385217514 bits at `L=8,M=2`.  This is a valid approximate-strong
alternative when that exposure is mandatory, but it increases the certified
radius relative to exact weight 32.

## 5. Route decision and implementation boundary

The three requested routes compare as follows.

| route | rigorous status | minimal unresolved change |
|---|---|---|
| retain current quadratic `q_perf` and use joint accepted multi-forks | not closed | accepted-fork conditioning and short Bezout coefficients are not supplied by the current interface |
| retain current quadratic `q_perf` and extract in one CRT component | not closed for the repository relation | a component kernel is not a short whole-ring SIS witness; standard CRT lifting has no certified centered shortness |
| replace it by `q_4` | challenge theorem proved | port the arithmetic backend and rerun completeness, centered-gate, and SIS certification |

The third route is the smallest *proof-interface* change that yields a full
unit-difference theorem while retaining a 50-bit modulus and a low challenge
norm.  It is not a small implementation patch.  The current Rust backend uses
64 quadratic slots represented by two length-64 transforms and quadratic
extension arithmetic.  This candidate needs 32 quartic slots represented by
four length-32 transforms and quartic extension arithmetic.

Accordingly, none of the following is inherited without a new implementation
and new runs:

- the current performance benchmark;
- PB/FB/NB calibration;
- centered-modulus certification;
- Euclidean-SIS/commitment estimator outputs.

An old-bound transfer screen in the JSON only locates likely gate failures.  It
is explicitly not a calibration or security certificate.  The new modulus is
slightly smaller than current `q_perf`, and several existing installed bounds
already exceed `q_4/2`; geometry redesign is therefore required before this
candidate can be called a closed repository performance line.
